The Scope Is the License

What a Colorado LLP can do — and the lines they can’t cross

The full C.R.C.P. 207.1 map: every authorized matter, every courtroom power, and every carve-out — the exam tests these lines relentlessly.

The scope IS the license. Everything a Colorado LLP may do — and the exact lines they may not cross — lives in C.R.C.P. 207.1. The exam tests it relentlessly, and in practice, crossing a line is the unauthorized practice of law. Here is the complete map.

In scope — an LLP may represent clients in:

  • Dissolution of marriage or civil union — pre- and post-decree
  • Legal separation and declaration of invalidity
  • Initial allocation and modification of parental responsibilities
  • Child support — establishment, enforcement, and modification
  • Remedial contempt tied to in-scope matters
  • Civil protection orders
  • Name changes and adult gender-designation changes
  • Two-party parentage cases

And in those matters, an LLP may:

  • Advise the client and prepare, sign, and file documents
  • Appear at hearings; examine witnesses; make arguments and objections
  • Answer the court’s questions and address the court; assist in mediation

The rule’s comment directs that the authorized scope be liberally construed to include all ordinary and reasonable actions within it.

Out of scope — the carve-outs (attorney required)

  • QDROs and division documents for business assets or commercial property
  • Prenuptial and postnuptial agreements — preparation or litigation
  • Punitive contempt under C.R.C.P. 107
  • Disputes over whether a common-law marriage exists or when it formed
  • Complex trust issues — beneficiary status, trust value or income disputes
  • Registration of foreign (out-of-state) orders
  • Parentage cases involving more than two parents; certain ICWA-related disputes
  • Collateral issues needing outside expertise: immigration, criminal, bankruptcy, and similar
The rule the exam loves most: when an out-of-scope issue appears mid-case, the LLP advises the client to get an attorney for that piece — and keeps the in-scope work under C.R.C.P. 207.1(i). Refer the piece, keep the case.
